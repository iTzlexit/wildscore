# Promotion

## The Kruger YouTube channel

### The strategy in one line

**Ask for their opinion, not for their audience.** The deal comes later, if at
all.

A first email that opens with a business proposal reads as marketing and gets
deleted — creators get several a week. A first email that asks a Kruger expert
for their view on a Kruger thing is flattering, costs them nothing to say yes
to, and starts an actual relationship. If they like the app they will bring up
promoting it themselves, and that conversation is worth ten times the one you
would have started.

There is also a real reason to want their opinion, which is what makes this
honest rather than a tactic: **the rarity tiers have never been reviewed by
anybody who knows Kruger properly.** The entire scoring system rests on them.
People who are in the park every week are the right reviewers, and nobody else
we know is better placed.

### Do not, in the first email

- Mention money, promo codes, revenue share, or "partnership"
- Attach the APK — it looks like spam and Gmail may strip it
- Send a wall of text about features
- Say "I hope this email finds you well"

### The draft

Subject lines, pick one:

- **A Kruger game my family has played for 30 years — now an app, and I'd love your eyes on it**
- **Built a Kruger scorecard app — would you test it and tell me the animal rankings are wrong?**

---

Hi [names],

I've been watching [channel] for [however long] — [one specific, real detail:
an episode, a sighting, something they said. This sentence is the whole reason
the email gets read, so it has to be true and it has to be specific. A generic
compliment reads worse than no compliment].

We've been going to Kruger since I was a kid, and the whole way there my family
played a scorecard game — everyone in the car competing to spot animals first,
rarer animals worth more points. It was a scrap of paper on the dashboard and it
kept four kids staring out of the window for six hours.

I'm a software developer, so I've spent the last few months building it properly
as an app. It's called Wild Score. You add whoever is in the car, tap a name when
somebody shouts, and pick the animal — points land on them based on how hard the
animal is to find. An impala is 5, a pangolin is 2,000. At the end of the day the
highest score wins, and every animal the car saw joins your permanent collection.

It works with no signal, which was the whole design problem — everything is
bundled into the app, 96 species with photographs, and it even names the road you
found something on from GPS alone.

Here's what I'd genuinely value from you. **I've had to rank every animal by how
hard it is to find in Kruger, and I've got no way of knowing if I've got it
right.** Is a serval really rarer than a caracal? Should a honey badger be worth
more? You two are in the park constantly and I'm not — if you'd be willing to
have a look and tell me where I've got it wrong, that would be worth more to me
than any amount of guessing.

Anything else you notice would be welcome too. I want to put it on the Play Store
soon and I'd rather hear what's wrong with it from someone who knows Kruger than
from a stranger's one-star review.

Happy to send it over and walk you through installing it — takes about two
minutes.

Either way, thanks for the videos. [Second specific detail if there's a natural
one.]

[Name]

---

### Then

**If they say yes** — send the APK with short installation instructions. Do not
send a feature list. Let them find it.

**Ask one specific question** rather than "what do you think" — an open question
gets a polite non-answer. "Are the rankings right?" gets a real one.

**Wait for them to like it** before any deal talk. If they mention promoting it,
that is the moment.

### The deal, when it comes up

Should you offer one? **Yes, but not first, and keep it simple.**

The usual shape for an app at this size:

- **A promo code giving their audience a discount** — say 20% off
- **A share of every sale that comes through it** — 20–30% is normal for a
  creator deal at this scale

On R250, a 20% discount makes it R200, and a 25% share is R50 to them per sale.
You keep R150 before Play's 15% cut.

Two things to know before promising anything:

**Google Play promo codes are limited.** You get a fixed allocation of one-time
codes per quarter (in the hundreds, not thousands), and they are single-use.
"Everyone in the audience gets 20% off" is not directly possible — what works is
either a batch of codes for a giveaway, or a **regional sale** timed to the video.

**Play does not track referrals for you.** There is no built-in "this sale came
from that creator". You would have to work it out from install referrer data or
simply agree a flat fee, or a share of the sales in the week following the video.
**A flat fee is much simpler and much less likely to end in an argument** about
whose sale was whose.

So the honest options, easiest first:

1. **Flat fee for a mention.** Clean, no tracking, no dispute.
2. **A batch of free codes** for them to give away, plus a flat fee.
3. **Revenue share on a time window** — "25% of sales in the two weeks after the
   video". Needs trust on both sides but no technical work.
4. **Proper referral tracking.** Real work, and not worth building for one
   creator.

**Do not build referral tracking before you have a single creator saying yes.**
